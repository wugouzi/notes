        If putting LayerNorms in residual streams is bad.. Why not post-norm outside the stream?

        Recent models: Grok, Gemma 2. Olmo 2 only does non-residual post norm

        LayerNorm: normalizes the mean and variance across \(d_{model}\)
        \begin{equation*}
        y=\frac{x-E[x]}{\sqrt{Var[x]+\epsilon}}*\gamma+\beta
        \end{equation*}

        GPT-3/2/1, OPT, GPT-J

        RMSNorm - does not subtract mean or add a bias term
        \begin{equation*}
        y=\frac{x}{\sqrt{\norm{x}_2^2+\epsilon}}*\gamma
        \end{equation*}

        LLaMA-family, PaLM, Chinchilla, T5

        Explanaion: faster and just as good
        * no mean calculation
        * fewer parameters

         Modern transformers don't have bias term: memory and optimization stability


         RECAP:
         Basically everyone does non-residual norm (often prenorm)
         * Intuition – keep the good parts of residual connections
         * Observations – nicer gradient propagation, fewer spike
        * Some people add a second norm outside the residual stream

        Most people do RMSnorm
        * In practice, works as well as LayerNorm
        * But, has fewer parameters to move around, which saves on wallclock time
        * People more generally drop bias terms since the compute/param tradeoffs are not great.


        ReLU: original transformer, T5, Gopher, Chinchilla, OPT
        \begin{equation*}
        FF(x)=\max(0,xW_1)W_2
        \end{equation*}
         

        GeLU: GPT1/2/3, GPTJ, GPT-Neox, Bloom
        \begin{align*}
        FF(x)&=GELU(xW_1)W_2\\
        GELU(x)&=x\Phi(x)
        \end{align*}

        gated activations:
        \begin{equation*}
        FF_{ReGLU}(x)=(\max(0,xW_1)\otimes xV)W_2
        \end{equation*}

        GeGLU: T5 v1.1, mT5, LaMDA, Phi3, Gemma2/3/4
        \begin{equation*}
        FFN_{GEGLU}(x,W,V,W_2)=(GELU(xW)\otimes xV)W_2
        \end{equation*}

        SwiGLU (swish is \(x sigmoid(x)\)) LLaMa 1/2/3, Mistral, most models post 2023
        \begin{equation*}
        FFN_{SwiGLU}(x,W,V,W_2)=(Switch(xW)\otimes xV)W_2
        \end{equation*}

        Many variations in position embeddings

        Absolute embeddings: GPT1/2/3
        \begin{equation*}
        Embed(x,i)=v_x+u_i
        \end{equation*}

        Relative embedding: add a vector to the attention computation
        \begin{equation*}
        e_{ij}=\frac{x_iW^Q(x_jW^K+a_{ij}^K)^T}{\sqrt{d_z}}
        \end{equation*}

        RoPE: most 2024+ model

        Motivation: a *relative* position embedding should be some \(f(x,i)\) s.t.
        \begin{equation*}
        \la f(x,i),f(y,j)\ra=g(x,y,i-j)
        \end{equation*}

        How can we solve this problem?
        * We want our embeddings to be invariant to absolute position
        * We know that inner products are invariant to arbitrary rotation.
