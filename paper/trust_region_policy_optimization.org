* Introduction
        Most algorithms for policy optimization can be classified into three broad categories:
        1. policy iteration methods, which alternate between estimating the value function under the current
           policy and improving the policy
        2. policy gradient methods, which use an estimator of the gradient of the expected return (total
           reward) obtained from sample trajectories (and which, as we later discuss, have a close connection
           to policy iteration)
        3. derivative-free optimization methods, such as the cross-entropy method (CEM) and covariance matrix
           adaptation (CMA), which treat the return as a black box function to be optimized in terms of the
           policy parameters

        
        In this article, we first prove that minimizing a certain surrogate objective function guarantees
        policy improvement with non-trivial step sizes. Then we make a series of approximations to the
        theoretically-justified algorithm, yielding a practical algorithm, which we call trust region policy
        optimization (TRPO).

        
* Preliminaries
        Consider an infinite-horizon discounted Markov decision process (MDP), defined by the tuple
        \((\cals,\cala,P,r,\rho_0,\gamma)\), where \(\cals\) is a finite set of states, \(\cala\) is a finite set of actions,
        \(P:\cals\times\cala\times\cals\to\R\) is the transition probability distribution, \(r:\cals\to\R\) is the reward function,
        \(\rho_0:\cals\to\R\) is the distribution of the initial state \(s_0\), and \(\gamma\in(0,1)\) is the discount factor.

        #+LATEX: \wu{
        "infinite-horizon": allow infinite interaction

        "discounted": future rewards are worth less than immediate rewards. The return function at time \(t\) is
        \begin{equation*}
        G_t=r_t+\gamma r_{t+1}+\gamma^2r_{t+2}+\gamma^3r_{t+3}+\cdots
        \end{equation*}
        #+LATEX: }

        Let
        \(\pi\)
        denote a stochastic policy \(\pi:\cals\times\cala\to[0,1]\), and let \(\eta(\pi)\) denote its expected discounted reward:
        \begin{equation*}
        \eta(\pi)=\E_{s_0,a_0,\dots}\left[\sum_{t=0}^\infty\gamma^tr(s_t)\right]
        \end{equation*}
        where
        \begin{equation*}
        s_0\sim\rho_0(s_0),a_t\sim\pi(a_t|s_t),s_{t+1}\sim P(s_{t+1}|s_t,a_t)
        \end{equation*}

        We will use the following standard definitions for *state-action value function* \(Q_\pi\), the *value function*
        \(V_{\pi}\) and the *advantage function* \(A_\pi\)
        \begin{align*}
        &Q_\pi(s_t,a_t)=\E_{s_{t+1},a_{t+1},\dots}\left[\sum_{l=0}^\infty\gamma^lr(s_{t+l})\right]\\
        &V_\pi(s_t)=\E_{a_t,s_{t+1},\dots}\left[\sum_{l=0}^\infty\gamma^lr(s_{t+l})\right]\\
        &A_\pi(s,a)=Q_\pi(s,a)-V_\pi(s)\text{ where }\\
        &\quad\quad a_t\sim\pi(a_t|s_t),s_{t+1}\sim P(s_{t+1}|s_t,a_t)\text{ for }t\ge 0
        \end{align*}

        The following useful identity expresses the expected return of another policy \(\hat{\pi}\) in terms of the
        advantage over \(\pi\), accumulated over timesteps
        \begin{equation*}
        \eta(\tilpi)=\eta(\pi)+\E_{s_0,a_0,\dots\sim\tilpi}\left[\sum_{i=0}^\infty\gamma^tA_\pi(s_t,a_t)\right]
        \end{equation*}

        Let \(\rho_\pi\) be the *(unnormalized) discounted visitation frequencies*:
        \begin{equation*}
        \rho_\pi(s)=P(s_0=s)+\gamma P(s_1=s)+\gamma^2P(s_2=s)+\dots
        \end{equation*}
        where \(s_0\sim\rho_0\) and the actions are chosen accoding to \(\pi\).

        #+LATEX: \wu{
        Suppose \(X\) and \(Y\) are two different random variables. Then
        \begin{align*}
        \E[X+Y]&=\sum_x\sum_y (x+y)p_{X,Y}(x,y)=\sum_x\sum_y  xp_{X,Y}(x,y)+\sum_x\sum_yyp_{X,Y}(x,y)\\
        &=\sum_xxP(X=x)+\sum_yyP(Y=y)\\
        &=\E[X]+\E[Y]
        \end{align*}
        #+LATEX: }

        We can rewrite \(\eta(\tilpi)\) as
        \begin{align}
        \eta(\tilpi)&=\eta(\pi)+\sum_{t=0}^\infty\sum_sP(s_t=s|\tilpi)\sum_a\tilpi(a|s)\gamma^tA_\pi(s,a)\notag\\
        &=\eta(\pi)+\sum_s\sum_{t=0}^\infty\gamma^tP(s_t=s|\tilpi)\sum_a\tilpi(a|s)A_\pi(s,a)\notag\\
        &=\eta(\pi)+\sum_s\rho_{\tilpi}(s)\sum_{a}\tilpi(a|s)A_\pi(s,a)\label{eq2}
        \end{align}
        This equation implies that any policy update \(\pi\to\tilpi\) that has a nonnegative expected advantage at *every*
        state \(s\), i.e., \(\sum_a\tilpi(a|s)A_\pi(s,a)\ge 0\), is guaranteed to increase the policy performance \(\eta\), or leave
        it constant in the case that the expected advantage is zero everywhere.

        This implies the classic result that the update performed by exact policy iteration, which uses the
        deterministic policy
        \begin{equation*}
        \tilpi(s)=\argmax_aA_\pi(s,a),
        \end{equation*}
        
        improves the policy if there is at least one state-action pair with a positive advantage value and
        nonzero state visitation probability, otherwise the algorithm has converged to the optimal policy.

        However, in the approximate setting, it will typically be unavoidable, due to estimation and
        approximation error, that there will be some states \(s\) for which the expected advantage is negative,
        that is, \(\sum_a\tilpi(a|s)A_\pi(s,a)<0\). The complex dependency of \(\rho_{\tilpi}(s)\) on \(\tilpi\) makes Equation [[eqref:eq2]]  difficult to
        optimize directly. Instead, we introduce the following local approximation to \(\eta\):
        \begin{equation*}
        L_\pi(\tilpi)=\eta(\pi)+\sum_s\rho_\pi(s)\sum_a\tilpi(a|s)A_\pi(s,a)
        \end{equation*}

        If we have a parameterized policy \(\pi_\theta\), where \(\pi_\theta(a|s)\) is a differentiable function of the parameter vector
        \(\theta\), then \(L_\pi\) matches \(\eta\) to first order. That is
        \begin{gather}
        L_{\pi_{\theta_0}}(\pi_{\theta_0})=\eta(\pi_{\theta_0})\notag\\
        \nabla_\theta L_{\pi_{\theta_0}}(\pi_\theta)|_{\theta=\theta_0}=\nabla_\theta\eta(\pi_\theta)|_{\theta=\theta_0}\label{eq4}
        \end{gather}

        Equation [[eqref:eq4]] implies that a sufficiently small step \(\pi_{\theta_0}\to\tilpi\) that improves
        \(L_{\pi_{\theta_{old}}}\) will also improve \(\eta\), but does not give us any guidance on how big of a step to take

        To address this issue, [[cite:&645531.656005]] proposed a policy updating scheme called *convervative
        policy interation*, for which they could provide explicit lower bounds on the improvement of \(\eta\). To
        define the conservative policy interation update, let \(\pi_{old}\) denote the current policy, and let
        \(\pi'=\argmax_{\pi'}L_{\pi_{old}}(\pi')\). The new policy \(\pi_{new}\) was defined to be the following mixture
        \begin{equation*}
        \pi_{new}(a|s)=(1-\alpha)\pi_{old}(a|s)+\alpha\pi'(a|s)
        \end{equation*}
        And we have the following lower bound
        \begin{equation}
        \label{eq6}
        \eta(\pi_{new})>L_{\pi_{old}}(\pi_{new})-\frac{2\epsilon\gamma}{(1-\gamma)^2}\alpha^2
        \end{equation}
        where \(\epsilon=\max_s\abs{\E_{a\sim\pi'(a|s)}[A_\pi(s,a)]}\)


* Monotonic Improvement Guarantee for General Stochastic Policies
        Our result is that the policy improvement bound in Equation [[eqref:eq6]] can be extended to general
        stochastic policies.

        The particular distance measure we use is the total variation divergence, which is defined by
        \(D_{TV}(p\parallel q)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_i\abs{p_i-q_i}\) for discrete probability densitiy \(p,q\). Define
        \begin{equation*}
        D_{TV}^{max}(\pi,\tilpi)=\max_sD_{TV}(\pi(\cdot|s)\parallel \tilpi(\cdot|s))
        \end{equation*}

        #+ATTR_LATEX: :options []
        #+BEGIN_theorem
        [[label:thm1]]
        Let \(\alpha=D_{TV}^{max}(\pi_{old},\pi_{new})\). Then the following bound holds
        \begin{equation*}
        \eta(\pi_{new})\ge L_{\pi_{old}}(\pi_{new})-\frac{4\epsilon\gamma}{(1-\gamma^2)}\alpha^2
        \end{equation*}
        where \(\epsilon=\max_{s,a}\abs{A_{\pi_{old}}(s,a)}\)
        #+END_theorem

        Next, we note the following relationship between the total variation divergence and the KL divergence
        \begin{equation*}
        D_{TV}(p\parallel q)^2\le D_{KL}(p\parallel q)
        \end{equation*}
        #+LATEX: \wu{
        \begin{equation*}
        D_{KL}(p\parallel q)=\sum_xp(x)\log\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}
        \end{equation*}
        #+LATEX: }
        
        Let \(D_{KL}^{max}(\pi,\tilpi)=\max_sD_{KL}(\pi(\cdot|s)\parallel \tilpi(\cdot|s))\). The following bound follows from Theorem [[ref:thm1]]
        \begin{equation}
        \label{eq9}
        \eta(\tilpi)\ge L_\pi(\tilpi)-CD^{max}_{KL}(\pi,\tilpi)
        \end{equation}
        where \(C=\frac{4\epsilon\gamma}{(1-\gamma)^2}\)

        It follows from Equation [[eqref:eq9]] that Algorithm ref:alg1 is guaranteed to generate a monotonically
        improving sequence of policies \(\eta(\pi_0)\le\eta(\pi_1)\le\dots\).
