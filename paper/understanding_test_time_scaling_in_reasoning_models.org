* Analysis of Test-Time Scaling in SoTA Reasoning LLMs
        Thinking process
        \begin{equation*}
        x\to z\to y
        \end{equation*}
        where \(x\) is the input prompt, \(z\sim\pi(\cdot|x)\) is the intermediate thinking trace, \(y\sim\pi(\cdot|x,z)\) is
        the final answer, and \(\pi\) denotes the language model. The RL objective for training reasoning model is:
        \begin{equation*}
        \max_\theta\E_{x,z\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot|x),y\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot|x,z)}[R(x,y)]
        \end{equation*}
        where \(\pi_\theta\) is the parameterized model and \(R(x,y)\) represents the true reward function.

        *Test time scaling*. The test-time scaling extends the thinking process as
        \begin{equation*}
        x\to z_1\to z_2\to\cdots\to z_k\to y
        \end{equation*}
        where, given the prompt \(x\), the first thinking step is generated as \(z_1\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot|x)\).
        Then, instead of directly producing the final answer, the model is instructed to continue thinking
        using special tokens such as “Wait” or “Think more”, denoted as \(p\) with the next thinking step sampled
        as \(z_2\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot|x,z_1,p)\). The process is continued iteratively for \(k\) steps, and the model generates the final
        response as \(y\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot|x,z_1,p,z_2,p,\dots,z_k)\)
        . For simplicity of representation, we just keep the thinking traces in the condition as
        \(y\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot|x,z_1,z_2,\dots,z_k)\)

* Illution of Test-Time Scaling: An Alternative Explanation
        Consider a simplified scenario. Assume the reward is fixed and unimodal, and the model samples answers
        from the policy distribution \(y\sim\pi(\cdot|x)\). Sepcifically, we assume \(\pi(y|x)=\caln(\mu_\pi,\sigma^2_\pi)\) and reward function
        follows a bell-curve as \(r(x,y)=\caln(\mu_r,\sigma_r^2)\). Hence, we can write
        \begin{align*}
        \E_{y\sim\pi(\cdot|x)}[r(x,y)]&=\int r(x,y)\cdot\pi(y|x)\cdot dy\\
        &=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(\sigma_r^2+\sigma_\pi^2)}}\cdot\exp\left( -\frac{(\mu_r-\mu_\pi)^2}{2(\sigma_r^2+\sigma_\pi^2)} \right)
        \end{align*}

        We adopt the Wait & Think more setup, where we suppress the generation of the end-of-thinking
        delimiter and append “Wait” to encourage the model for additional thinking. To measure the entropy of
        the policy, after appending each “Wait”, we sample multiple answers \(y\sim\pi(\cdot|z_{1:i},x)\),  where
        \(z_{1:i} =[z_1 , \dots, z_{i-1} , z_i ]\) denotes the cumulative sequence of thinking tokens
        generated up to the \(i\)th-“Wait”, \(z_1\) denotes the initial thinking trace generated with standard
        thinking, and \(x\) is the given prompt. We quantify policy uncertainty using the entropy of the distribution over final answers:
        \begin{equation*}
        \E_{y\sim\pi(\cdot|z_{1:i},x)}[-\log\pi(y|z_{1:i},x)]
        \end{equation*}
